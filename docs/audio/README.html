Two facts that shape every script in this folder

Voice binds to race, not to culture. A soldier picks his voice from his race's skin, and nothing in that path consults his culture. So Erebor, the Iron Hills and Ironpass all share one dwarf voice and cannot be told apart by ear, and Gondor, Rohan and Dale share the vanilla human race and therefore cannot have separate voices at all without new code. Plan a script per race, not per culture, and check which cultures fall under it before promising a distinct sound.

Half the races have no voice of their own. Seven of the fourteen custom races fall through to Calradian voices today: elf, orc, nazghul, cave_troll, hill_troll, saruman, sauron. An elf currently sounds like an Imperial levy.

Nazgûl, Saruman and Sauron are named individuals rather than troop lines. They belong with dialogue work, not with a combat voice set.

Before starting a new script

  1. Check how the race is currently bound, and whether it is bound at all. docs/features/kingdom-voices.md has the table.
  2. Pull the real slot list, do not guess the count. That race's sound groups come out of Main/_Module/ModuleData/module_sounds.xml and its slot mapping out of the matching lotr_<race>_voice_def.xml. Those two files decide how many lines the script needs.
  3. Copy the structure of the dwarf script. The block order (wordless first, cries last) exists to protect the actor's voice, and the appendix mapping stems to sound groups is what makes the files usable when they come back.
  4. If the script invents language, it needs its own lexicon alongside, split into sourced and invented the way the Khuzdul one is.
