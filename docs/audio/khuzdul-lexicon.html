TAOM Khuzdul Lexicon

The dwarvish used in TAOM's voice-over scripts, and the rules for adding to it.

Khuzdul is the Dwarves' own language. Tolkien published very little of it, perhaps a hundred words and a handful of phrases, because in the story the Dwarves guard it and almost never speak it in front of outsiders. That leaves a working problem: TAOM's dwarf voice set needs about forty spoken lines, and Tolkien's corpus does not contain forty lines' worth of vocabulary. So most of what a TAOM dwarf shouts has to be built.

This page separates the two halves so nobody has to guess which is which. Part 1 is Tolkien's, with a source for every entry. Part 2 is ours, marked as ours. Part 3 is the pronunciation key the voice actor actually reads.

The rule that governs additions: build only from Part 1. Never import film Khuzdul.

The Khuzdul spoken in the Peter Jackson films is neo-Khuzdul written by David Salo. It is his creative work, not Tolkien's published vocabulary, and TAOM has no claim on it. Tolkien's own published words are a different matter: short invented words and names quoted from a book are the same kind of reference as the place names and character names TAOM already uses throughout. Our own coinages are ours.

So: a new word is either sourced to Tolkien, or built by us from Tolkien's roots and patterns and recorded in Part 2. There is no third category, and "I saw it in the film" is not a source.

What the shape of the language tells us

Four patterns visible in the table above, which are what Part 2 is built on:

  • Roots are consonant skeletons. Kh-Z-D gives both Khuzd and Khazâd; the vowels between the consonants carry the grammar. This is why dwarvish words feel blocky and front-loaded.
  • Plurals lengthen a vowel inside the word rather than adding an ending. rukhs becomes rakhâs, Khuzd becomes Khazâd. There is no dwarvish -s.
  • In a compound the describing word comes first. gabil-gathol is great-fortress, sigin-tarâg is long-beards, baraz-inbar is red-horn.
  • Possession runs the other way. Baruk Khazâd is axes-of-the-Dwarves, the thing owned first. And a sentence needs no verb: Khazâd ai-mênu is just "Dwarves upon-you".

Part 2: TAOM coinages

Every word below is invented for TAOM. None of it is Tolkien's and none of it is film Khuzdul. Each one is built on the patterns above: consonants Tolkien actually used, the compound order he used, plurals in long â.

Two devices are ours and used consistently, so learn them once:

  • na- reverses a word. shakil is "loose", nashkil is "hold your shot". zurun is "mount", nazurun is "get down". It gives paired orders that sound related, which is how real command vocabulary behaves.
  • -zai marks a formation. Three of the eight formation orders are rank shapes, and they share the ending: sigin-zai, zirak-zai, zûn-zai. The other five formations are deliberately single distinct words instead, so the whole set does not rhyme.

Part 3: how to say it

For the voice actor. You do not need to understand the grammar; you need six sounds and one stress rule.

The six sounds that are not English

WrittenSay itExample
khThe scrape at the end of Scottish loch or German Bach. Not a k. If you cannot get it, a hard k with grit on it is acceptable and better than a soft oneKhazâd, bakhar
thAs in thin, never as in thiszathar, thagûn
shAs in shiprakhâs, shakil
zAs in zoo, buzzed, never softened to an szirak, guzâm
rTapped or lightly rolled, as in Scots. Never the swallowed English rbaruk, turkun
gAlways hard, as in gate, never as in gemgabil, guzrak

Vowels

Short vowels are pure and clipped: a as in father but short, i as in sit, u as in put, e as in set, o as in hot.

A circumflex means hold it. â î û ê are the same vowels but roughly twice as long, and they are where the weight of a dwarvish word sits. Khazâd is kha-ZAAD. rakhâs is ra-KHAAS. Do not shorten them; the long vowel is most of what makes the language sound dwarvish rather than orcish.

Stress

Stress the long vowel. If the word has a circumflex, that syllable takes the beat: Khazâd, rakhâs, thagûn, guzâm.

If there is no long vowel, stress the first syllable: BAR-uk, ZIR-ak, NAKH-ud, TUR-kun.

Two habits to fight

  • Release the final consonant. English lets word-final d, k, t die in the throat. Khuzdul does not. Khazâd ends on an audible d. Baruk ends on an audible k. Across a battlefield that final consonant is the whole difference between one order and another.
  • Do not soften the clusters. Zirak-zai, Thagûn-khadal and Nashkil open hard and stay hard. There is nothing graceful about this language; it was made for shouting in a tunnel.

If you want one reference for the whole register
