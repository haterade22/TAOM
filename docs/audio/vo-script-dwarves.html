Dwarf Voice Script

TAOM (Tales From the Age of Men). Culture: Erebor. 200 takes.

One voice set covers every dwarf in the game: Erebor, the Iron Hills, Ironpass, and the Blacklock outlaws. Whatever you record here is what every dwarf on the field sounds like, on both sides of every battle.

Read first: the recording guide for the format, the file naming and the session order. the Khuzdul lexicon for how to say the dwarvish, which is most of this script.

Total: 200 takes. 193 of them are the combat set; 7 are the addendum at the end. On top of that there is a dialogue block and a narration block, listed after the combat set, which are separate jobs and can be a separate session.

Who these men are

Dwarves of the Lonely Mountain and the Iron Hills. Miners, smiths and gate wardens who fight in close order behind shields, with axes, mattocks and crossbows. They are short, immensely strong, and they do not break. The game gives their kingdom the trait "Dwarven Stubbornness" and it is the right word for the whole culture: they are slow to build the wall and the wall holds until the mountain falls.

The register

Deep, chesty, ground-down. Weight rather than volume. These are men who work underground and shout over hammers, so the voice sits low and pushes from the gut, never from the throat. Gravel is welcome. Refinement is not. When in doubt, go lower and heavier than feels natural and let the microphone take it.

Not this

Not comic. Not a stage Scotsman. Not the boisterous drinking-song dwarf. There is humour in these men but it is dry and it is not in the battle lines. If a take sounds cheerful, do it again.

Accent

Bring whatever gives you the deepest, most weighted chest voice. If that is Northern English, Scots or Yorkshire, use it. The one thing to avoid is a bright, forward, well-spoken sound. The vowels want to be dark.

About the dwarvish

Most of these lines are in Khuzdul, the Dwarves' own language. You do not need to learn it. Every line prints its pronunciation and its meaning, and the meaning is there so you can act the line rather than recite it.

Baruk Khazâd! Khazâd ai-mênu!
BAR-uk kha-ZAAD! kha-ZAAD eye-MAY-noo!

"Axes of the Dwarves! The Dwarves are upon you!" Tolkien's own line, and the reference for the whole script. Everything else should sound like it came from the same throat.

A circumflex means hold the vowel. Khazâd is kha-ZAAD, not kha-zad. The long vowels carry the weight and they are most of what makes this sound dwarvish.

Every order and formation line also gets one English take. That is the last take in each group, marked Take 04 English in the tables. It is insurance: if the dwarvish turns out to be unreadable in a live battle we swap to English without booking you again. Record it flat and functional, same register, no performance.

Block 1: wordless and effort 5 takes each 40 takes

Record this block first, while your voice is fresh and undamaged. These are body sounds, not performances. Do the thing; do not portray it. If a take sounds like acting, it is wrong.

Vary them: different pitch, different length, different amount of air. Three of the five should sound like three different men.

FileWhat it isLimitDirection
dwarf_gruntSwinging an axe4 sShort exhale of effort on impact. The sound of a heavy tool meeting something. Not a shout. Half of these should be almost inaudible, a working man's hnh
dwarf_painTaking a wound4 sInvoluntary. The air punched out of him. Somewhere between a bark and a grunt. Two of the five should be bad, the kind that catches at the end
dwarf_deathDying8 sNot a scream. A dwarf goes down hard and angry, and the sound cuts off rather than fading. Give one that is defiant to the last breath and one that is just the body giving out
dwarf_jumpJumping down or across4 sThe grunt of landing. Brief, compressed
dwarf_stunStaggered by a heavy blow4 sDazed. A groan with the wind gone, no words in it, no clean edge
dwarf_fearNear something that frightens him4 sRare in play, so it lands as a moment. A caught breath and a low sound of dread. Not a scream, not a whimper. This is a man who does not frighten easily being frightened
dwarf_climbHauling up a siege ladder4 sSustained effort, teeth gritted. Heavier than the axe grunt and longer
dwarf_focusBracing, drawing, steadying4 sA held breath and a low growl of concentration. The sound before the effort, not during it

Block 2: orders 3 dwarvish + 1 English 80 takes

A captain shouting across open ground with a battle running. Full projection, chest not throat, every final consonant landed hard. These are the lines that must carry over other men shouting. All 8 second limit.

Vary the three dwarvish takes properly. One should be a clean command, one strained and urgent as though things are going badly, one flat and hard as though this is the fourth time he has given it.

Block 5: cries, victory and dismay 5 takes each 25 takes

Record this block last. It is the most destructive material in the script and a tired throat suits it. There is no English safety take here; these are meant to be dwarvish.

Here the five takes are five different lines rather than five readings of one.

dwarf_warcry 8 s

Unprompted, mid-battle, and again when a rally goes up. A man making noise because he has decided to.

A multiplayer attack call, and the nastiest line in the script. Both words are Tolkien's.

Five readings, and let them differ. One cold and instructional. One savage. One almost weary, the sound of a man who has done this many times. Dwarves and Orcs have a long history and none of it is abstract to him.

Fires when his formation's morale breaks and men around him start running. Dismay, not fear.

Held long on the vowel. This is a man watching something he cannot stop. Two of the five should have no word in them at all, just the sound of it.

Dialogue block

Separate job, separate session, and worth reading the caveat first.

This is normal spoken dialogue: a conversation with the player, indoors, no shouting. Composed, close-mic, conversational. It is a completely different instrument from everything above, which is why it is not mixed into the combat blocks.

The caveat: the game cannot play this yet. Spoken dialogue in Bannerlord runs through a separate system from combat barks, and that system currently has no idea TAOM's cultures exist. Making it work is a code change on our side that has not been done. Recording these now means the audio is ready when it lands; it does not mean anyone will hear it this month. Say the word if you would rather wait.

Gimli, son of Glóin

The only canonical Tolkien character in this block. Proud, blunt, spoiling for something worth doing. Younger and hungrier than the Gimli most people picture; this is a dwarf who has not yet made his name and is impatient about it. The boasting is real, not ironic, but there is warmth under it.

Four lines, spoken across one conversation. Three takes each.

FileLine
gimli_backstory_a"I am Gimli, son of Glóin, a dwarf of the mountain halls. My axe seeks worthy battles."
gimli_backstory_b"The forge grows cold when there are no deeds to inspire the songs. I seek glory and gold, as any proper dwarf should!"
gimli_backstory_c"My father spoke of great adventures in his youth. Now it's my turn to prove that dwarf steel and dwarf courage remain unmatched in all the realm."
gimli_backstory_d"Boasting? Ha! My axe has felled more foes than you've had hot meals! But I seek companions worthy of a dwarf's loyalty. Perhaps you'll do."

Line d is a reply to being called a braggart. It should land as amused rather than offended, and the last sentence is the one that matters: he is sizing the player up.

The four other dwarf companions

TAOM has four more dwarf companions, each with the same four-line recruitment conversation: Rukumazig-khamîz the Smith, Thanor, Whitegoat the Pathfinder, and Hatérade.

Flagged for a decision before recording, not a problem with the writing. These four are tributes to the people who built the mod, and their dialogue is written as such: it talks about programming, interface design and building the world itself. Hatérade introduces himself as "architect of this very realm you walk". Voicing them straight would put four openly out-of-world characters in the same breath as Gimli, and it is worth deciding whether that is the intent before spending session time on it.

Narration block

The one block that is immediately usable, because it needs no game code at all. Not shouted, not conversational. This is a storyteller's register: slow, weighted, spoken to a room. Three takes each.

FileText
narr_erebor_culture"The noble Dwarves of Erebor are renowned for their craftsmanship, resilience, and pride. From their halls in the Lonely Mountain they forge mighty weapons, and treasures of unmatched beauty. Clad in mail and armed with axes, they defend their kin with steadfast courage, seeking to reclaim their legacy and preserve their heritage against all who threaten it."
narr_erebor_kingdom"The Lonely Mountain, reclaimed. Its people are master smiths, slow to raise a wall, and slower still to let one fall. They are stubborn past reason, loyal past death, and they have never yet been driven from a hall they meant to hold."
narr_erebor_trailer"Under the mountain, the forges are lit again. The Kingdom Under the Mountain has a King, and the King has an army. Baruk Khazâd."

The last three words of the trailer read are dwarvish: BAR-uk kha-ZAAD, "Axes of the Dwarves". Drop into it rather than shouting it. It should sound like a threat being made quietly.
