feat(mission-perf): v2.0.32 - log clip memory against the 12 MiB budget

The engine loads animation clips on demand and holds their data
against a 12 MiB budget: once a load takes the total past 15 MiB, it
schedules a pass that frees idle clips toward the budget. A worker
that then needs an evicted clip waits for it to load again, a suspect
for battle frame spikes. This probe measures whether TAOM battles
reach that budget, which decides between making TAOM's busiest clips
resident, raising the budget, or neither.

"Enable Animation Clip Memory Probe" (MCM, Battle Load Diagnostics,
Mission Performance) is on by default and needs no restart. Once per
game session it finds the engine's clip byte counter and budget by a
signature check, a scan of about 10 MB of the engine's code under the
first mission's loading screen. It only reads: if the signature, the
section checks or the 12582912-byte budget do not match, it turns
itself off and says why. It is left out of the co-op settings
fingerprint, like the frame-time heartbeat.

In each mission it reads the counter once a second and writes to
taom_debug.log:
- "[AnimMem] mission start", then a line every 5 s: loaded KB, percent
  of the budget, whether a clip is loading, drops (a proxy for
  evictions), the window's minimum and maximum, and the samples that
  saw a clip loading;
- at mission end, a line for the samples since the last 5 s line and
  an "[AnimMem] summary:" line (peak, samples at or above 90 percent,
  drops, loading samples);
- "[AnimMem] armed", "disabled", "off" or "stopped" lines for each
  state change.
Readings between 100 and 125 percent of the budget are normal, since
eviction starts only past 15 MiB, and a pass can leave the total above
12 MiB while clips are in use or loading.

The cost to every player while it is on: one counter read and one
native walk over the clip records each second, and one INFO line every
5 s (about 110 KB of log per hour of missions). MCM keeps a player's
saved value, so changing the default later means renaming the
setting; whether it stays on for a public release is still open.
